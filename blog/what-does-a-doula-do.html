What does a doula actually do?

By Eva Levinson · Updated May 2026

The word doula comes up a lot in pregnancy circles, but most expectant parents I meet have a slightly hazy idea of what we actually do — and crucially, what we don't do.

This is the honest version. Written by a Doula UK trained doula who also works as a Postnatal Midwifery Assistant in an NHS hospital. So I've seen this from both sides.

The short definition

A doula is a trained, non-medical professional who provides emotional, physical and informational support during pregnancy, birth and the early postnatal period.

We don't replace your midwife or obstetrician. We complement them — by being a continuous presence who knows you, knows your wishes, and stays with you when the clinical team rotates shifts.

What we DO do

  • Antenatal preparation — birth planning, hospital bag, knowing-your-options sessions, partner prep
  • Continuous birth support — present (or virtually present) through labour, advocating for your wishes
  • Postnatal support — feeding help, sleep guidance, light household help, and just being there in the muddy first weeks
  • Translating medical-speak — explaining what "failure to progress" actually means, what your options are, what the evidence says
  • Emotional anchor — for both you and your partner. Birth is intense. Having someone who's calm and trained is huge.

What we DON'T do

This is where most articles get vague. Let me be specific:

  • We don't deliver babies. That's a midwife's job. Doulas are not medical professionals.
  • We don't perform medical checks. No blood pressure, no foetal heart monitoring, no examinations.
  • We don't make decisions for you. We give you information and emotional support; you make the calls.
  • We don't replace your partner. A doula's role is to support BOTH of you. I'm there to make your partner more useful, not less needed.
  • We don't take sides against your medical team. Good doulas work with midwives, not against them.

Birth doula vs postnatal doula

These are different services, and many doulas (myself included) offer both:

Birth doula — supports you through pregnancy and labour. Antenatal sessions, on-call from 38 weeks, presence (or virtual presence) at the birth itself.

Postnatal doula — supports you in the first 6 weeks home with baby. Feeding help, sleep guidance, light cooking, mum-recovery checks, just being a calm extra pair of hands.

Many parents book both. I always think postnatal doula support is the most under-rated — you've never needed help more, and partners often go back to work after 2 weeks.

Does a doula reduce intervention?

The research says yes. A 2017 Cochrane review of 16 studies covering 17,000 births found that continuous labour support (which is what a doula provides):

  • Reduced the likelihood of caesarean by 25%
  • Reduced use of any pain medication by 11%
  • Increased likelihood of spontaneous vaginal birth
  • Improved how parents felt about their birth experience

That's not magic. It's mostly that having a calm, knowledgeable continuous presence helps you feel safer, which helps your body do its job.

Do you need a doula?

Honestly — no parent needs a doula. People have been having babies without us for thousands of years.

But you might really benefit from one if:

  • It's your first baby and you're feeling unprepared
  • You had a difficult previous birth and want this one to feel different
  • Your partner can't be there, or feels nervous about being your only support
  • You're planning a home birth or VBAC (vaginal birth after caesarean)
  • You don't have family nearby for the postnatal weeks
  • You're a single parent
  • You just want a calmer, more informed experience
